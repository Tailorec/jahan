# Plan: M3.5 `corpus` — what is actually in there, and what a field's value is worth

> Source PRD: `docs/prd/M3.5-corpus.md`
> Binding decisions: ADR 0017 (a grounded field says whether it was measured or extracted), ADR 0018 (calibrate against marginals rather than fusing persons), ADR 0019 (topology is generated, its parameters swept), ADR 0020 (the pre-flight has two stages), ADR 0016 (research instrument; no corpus row in the repository), ADR 0002 as amended (coverage), ADR 0014 as amended (catalog-constrained vocabulary). Where `FINAL_ARCH.md` still disagrees, the ADRs win.

## Architectural decisions

Durable across every phase:

- **Two ports, not one** — `CoresetSource` answers three questions from rows; `CoresetCatalog` answers coverage and counts from an index. They are separate because their cost profiles differ by four orders of magnitude, and because typing `preview()` to the catalog is what stops it growing a row read.
- **The pre-flight has two stages** — `preview()` says what the corpus holds and reads only the index; `assess()` says whether the draw is sound and needs rows. Neither calls a model except the natural-language step, which is bounded by the catalog.
- **A tier grades a claim, it never gates one** — `MEASURED` and `EXTRACTED` replace `GROUNDED`; a report carries the weakest tier among its gated attributes. The single refusal is `GateReference.CATEGORY_TARGETS` on attributes that are not `MEASURED`. Audiences and mixed sources are graded by the same rule rather than restricted, so one rule covers every surface.
- **Denominators are always reported** — "nobody was asked" and "nobody is like this" are different facts that look identical as a zero, and the first one is what hid this module's problem for three modules.
- **The adapter owns dataset knowledge** — packed encoding, override precedence, the source of a row, and the mapping from source and assignment type to a tier all live beside the thing that speaks to the dataset, as the packed format already does.
- **Nothing downloads implicitly** — a study needing an absent shard fails with the command that fetches it. Every cached file is verified against `manifest.json` on each use, not only at fetch, and the cache lives outside the repository.
- **No corpus row enters this repository** — a subset inherits the corpus's terms (ADR 0016), so decoder fixtures are generated in the real packed format and real-shard tests skip unless the shards are present locally. The suite stays offline and the socket refusal in `tests/conftest.py` is untouched.
- **Study parameters are tunable, defaulted and recorded** — `admissible_sources` joins `PopulationParameters` and travels on the manifest, like every other value that changes what a study measured.
- **Contract amendments come first** — nothing can carry a tier, an evidence grade or a preview that does not exist yet. `SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1.0.0 and unreleased, so pinned identities are re-pinned rather than migrated.
- **Test posture** — boundary tests through `preview`, `assess` and `build` only. The decoder is proven against a generated parquet; the real shards prove the adapter and are skipped when absent.

## Phase 1: `FieldOrigin` gains its tiers

**User stories**: 12

### What to build

The distinction every later phase reads. `GROUNDED` becomes `MEASURED` and `EXTRACTED`, so a survey answer and a model's reading of review text stop decoding to the same claim. This is a contract change with a wide, mechanical blast radius: `Population`'s invariants speak in terms of grounded attributes, module 3 sets origins during projection, and the pinned identities move.

Nothing consumes the new tier yet. The phase is finished when the shape exists, every existing behaviour is preserved under the new names, and the suite is green.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] `FieldOrigin` carries `MEASURED`, `EXTRACTED`, `SYNTHESIZED` and `CALIBRATED`, and no value named `GROUNDED` remains
- [x] A population's invariants are stated in terms of the tiers, and a gate on an attribute no carrier holds at a gateable tier is still refused
- [x] Projection assigns a tier to every field it takes from the corpus, defaulting to `MEASURED` for sources that declare no assignment type
- [x] The pinned identities in `tests/fixtures/hash_stability.json` are re-pinned, with the change recorded in the commit that moves them
- [x] The full suite passes under `-W error` with no behaviour change other than the renaming

## Phase 2: Evidence grade on the gate report

**User stories**: 13, 14

### What to build

The first thing to read a tier. A `GateReport` computes the weakest tier among the attributes it gated and carries it, the way it already computes its reference and its overall verdict — so a pass can never be read as stronger than its weakest evidence.

With that in place, one refusal becomes expressible: a gate may not claim `CATEGORY_TARGETS` on an attribute that is not `MEASURED`. "This population matches the measured category" is the engine's strongest distributional claim, and it must not rest on a model reading product reviews.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A gate report computes and carries the weakest tier among its gated attributes, and a supplied value contradicting the computation is refused
- [x] A report whose gates are all measured grades as measured; one extracted gate lowers the whole report
- [x] A gate judged against category targets on a non-measured attribute is refused, naming the attribute and its tier
- [x] A study declaring no audiences still gates against category targets when its attributes are measured
- [x] The grade survives a round trip through the population's serialized form

## Phase 3: The catalog seam and `preview()`

**User stories**: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6

### What to build

The first end-to-end answer to "what is in here?", running against the synthetic source so the shape is proven before any real data is involved.

`CoresetCatalog` answers coverage and counts without decoding rows. `preview()` turns a request into a report: per source, how many rows match, how many carry the attributes at all, and how many exist; which attributes are measured, extracted or absent; how many fields would be synthesized; which constraints the relaxation ladder would drop; and the resulting evidence grade.

The report always states all three counts, because a zero from an unasked question and a zero from an empirical fact are the same character otherwise.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A catalog answers coverage and counts for a set of attributes and sources, and the synthetic source implements it
- [x] `preview()` takes a catalog and never a source, so it cannot read a row
- [x] The preview reports matched, carrying and total counts per source, distinguishing an attribute nobody carries from one nobody matches
- [x] The preview names each requested attribute's tier per source, including absent
- [x] The preview states how many fields would be synthesized and the share that represents
- [x] The preview forecasts which relaxation rungs the request would climb, without drawing a sample
- [x] The preview reports the evidence grade the resulting population would carry

## Phase 4: Natural language to filters, bounded by coverage

**User stories**: 1, 5

### What to build

An audience described in words becomes predicates, with the catalog as the only vocabulary the model is given. A proposed filter naming an attribute with no coverage in any admissible source is refused rather than passed through to fail at sampling — the specific guard against a model proposing a field because the schema lists it.

The interpretation is shown back before anything runs, as ADR 0014 requires of anything drafted from the corpus.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] The request reaches the model through the existing chat port, and the suite exercises it with the fake
- [x] The model is given only the coverage table for the admissible sources as its permitted vocabulary
- [x] A proposed filter naming an unknown attribute, or one with zero coverage everywhere, is refused with the coverage that justified the refusal
- [x] A proposed value outside an attribute's vocabulary is refused rather than coerced
- [x] The interpreted filters are returned for confirmation before any draw
- [x] An interpretation that is confirmed produces exactly the predicates a hand-authored audience would

## Phase 5: The packed decoder

**User stories**: 7, 8, 9

### What to build

The dataset's packed format, decoded correctly, proven against a fixture this repository generates rather than a row it redistributes.

Three rules the codebook does not document and which each cost a wrong answer to learn: codes are indexed from zero, two to a byte, low nibble first; the null bitmap is the sole authority on presence and may be absent entirely, which means fully populated; and `attribute_overrides` supersede the code, always sit on code 0, and are always out of vocabulary. Override values that encode missingness fold to absent; values the enum cannot express are mapped or dropped, never admitted raw, because a closed vocabulary is what the gates and filters rest on.

The decoder checks itself: a decoded row whose populated count disagrees with the corpus's own fails loudly.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A generated parquet in the real packed format backs the tests, and no corpus row is committed
- [x] Codes decode from zero, two per byte, low nibble first, and a code at the vocabulary boundary decodes to its last value
- [x] A field marked missing in the bitmap decodes as absent even when its code is non-zero
- [x] A row with no bitmap decodes every field as present
- [x] An override supersedes its code, and a row whose override is missing-valued decodes that field as absent
- [x] An override carrying a value outside the vocabulary never reaches a decoded row as a raw string
- [x] A decoded row whose populated count disagrees with the corpus's own raises, naming the row

## Phase 6: `HfCoresetSource` — rows from real shards

**User stories**: 10, 11

### What to build

The corpus, readable. The decoder from phase 5 behind the existing source port, over shards fetched explicitly and verified on every use.

A row's source is derived from its identifiers, because the corpus records source only in its index and its metadata shape differs per source — a Wikidata id for one, a survey user id for another, nothing at all for the synthetic rows. The tier follows from the source and the assignment type together, since neither alone separates a survey answer from a model's reading.

Integration tests run against the shards cached locally and skip when they are absent, so the suite stays offline.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A study needing an absent shard fails with the command that fetches it, and nothing downloads implicitly
- [x] Every cached file is verified against the manifest's digest on each use, and a mismatch refuses the file
- [x] The cache lives outside the repository and is never written to by the suite
- [x] A row's source is derived from its identifiers and matches the manifest's source counts
- [x] A field's tier is derived from its source and assignment type, and a source with empty evidence still grades as measured
- [x] Integration tests decode real rows from each cached shard and skip cleanly when the shards are absent
- [x] The suite passes with no network access

## Phase 7: The index-backed catalog

**User stories**: 1, 2, 3, 4

### What to build

The catalog from phase 3, answered from the corpus's index instead of an in-memory source, so audience exploration costs a megabyte rather than four gigabytes. This is what makes the preview usable before any download decision.

The phase's real deliverable is a guarantee: a test that proves `preview()` reads no shard, however the catalog is backed.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] The catalog answers coverage and single-attribute counts without reading any shard
- [x] Conjunctions across attributes are answered from the index, and agree with the counts a row scan produces on a cached shard
- [x] A preview run against the index-backed catalog opens no shard file, asserted rather than assumed
- [x] The catalog's counts agree with the manifest's source totals
- [x] A preview against the real index reports the coverage this module was written for: the conditioning attributes that are absent, and from which sources

## Phase 8: `admissible_sources` and mixed-source grading

**User stories**: 15, 16

### What to build

Which sources a study may draw from becomes a recorded choice rather than an implicit one. A union across sources is permitted — it is often the only way to fill an audience — but never silent: the preview shows the resulting mix, and the population's grade falls to the weakest source in it.

Audience filters on extracted or synthetic attributes are permitted and labelled, not refused, so one grading rule covers gates, audiences and sources alike.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] `admissible_sources` is a study parameter with a default, carried on the manifest
- [x] A study restricted to one source draws from it alone, and a study naming several may draw from all of them
- [x] A population mixing sources grades to the weakest tier present
- [x] An audience filtering on a non-measured attribute is permitted, and the preview and the report both say so
- [x] Reaching a quota by admitting a weaker source is visible in the preview before the draw and in the report after it

## Phase 9: The two example studies

**User stories**: 17, 18

### What to build

Two studies with two distinct jobs.

The beverage study stays the offline quickstart and keeps needing no download, but its ontology is re-authored to be honest about what a synthetic source supplies — today it names attributes as though they were grounded.

The second study is the proof: an audience on the one source that measures demographics and attitudes on the same person, run end to end against real rows. Its audiences are grounded filters rather than projections, which is what makes it worth shipping as the real-data example.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] The quickstart runs offline, downloads nothing, and its ontology declares tiers matching what a synthetic source actually supplies
- [x] The real-data study previews, assesses and builds against cached shards, and skips when they are absent
- [x] Its audiences resolve through measured attributes, and its gate report grades as measured
- [x] Both studies are runnable from documented commands, and the real-data one states its download step
- [x] The real-data study's preview output appears in the docs as the worked example of reading coverage
